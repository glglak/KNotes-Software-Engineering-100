# Senior Engineer Reading Path

Move from writing good code to owning systems, reliability, trade-offs, mentoring, and broader engineering outcomes.

1. **A Philosophy of Software Design — John Ousterhout**  
   The central enemy of software design is complexity, so create deep modules with simple interfaces that hide difficult decisions.

2. **Designing Data-Intensive Applications — Martin Kleppmann & Chris Riccomini**  
   Understand databases, replication, partitioning, consistency, streams, and distributed-system trade-offs well enough to design systems rather than merely assemble technologies.

3. **Release It! — Michael T. Nygard**  
   Production systems must be designed to survive latency, dependency failure, cascading faults, overload, and all the ugly realities ignored by happy-path architecture diagrams.

4. **Unit Testing: Principles, Practices, and Patterns — Vladimir Khorikov**  
   Good tests verify observable behavior rather than implementation details, giving high confidence without making refactoring painful.

5. **Modern Software Engineering — David Farley**  
   Effective engineering applies fast feedback, experimentation, modularity, automation, and empirical learning so software can evolve safely.

6. **Domain-Driven Design — Eric Evans**  
   For complex business systems, make the domain model and shared language between engineers and experts the center of the architecture.

7. **Understanding Distributed Systems — Roberto Vitillo**  
   Distributed systems become easier to reason about once you understand networks, failures, replication, consistency, coordination, scalability, and observability from first principles.

8. **Accelerate — Nicole Forsgren, Jez Humble & Gene Kim**  
   High-performing engineering organizations simultaneously improve delivery speed and stability through measurable capabilities such as deployment frequency, lead time, recovery time, and change-failure rate.

9. **The Missing README — Chris Riccomini & Dmitriy Ryaboy**  
   Becoming an effective professional engineer requires skills that university rarely teaches, including code reviews, design docs, on-call work, debugging, collaboration, and career navigation.

10. **The Effective Engineer — Edmond Lau**  
   Engineering impact comes from maximizing leverage by prioritizing work that produces disproportionate long-term value.

11. **The Software Engineer’s Guidebook — Gergely Orosz**  
   Career progression from engineer to senior, lead, and staff levels requires increasing ownership, judgment, technical scope, communication, and organizational impact.

12. **Building Secure and Reliable Systems — Heather Adkins et al.**  
   Security and reliability should be designed together using least privilege, defense in depth, resilience, recoverability, and controlled change.
